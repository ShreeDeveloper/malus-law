Aim, Apparatus & Experimental Procedure

Aim

To verify the Law of Malus using a polarizer-analyzer pair and a Light Dependent Resistor (LDR) / photocell detector.

Apparatus

  • Optical bench with upright stands
  • Incandescent light source (S) and collimating convex lens (L)
  • Fixed Polarizer (P) and rotatable Analyzer (A) mounted on a 360° circular scale
  • LDR / Photocell sensor connected in series with a DC power supply and digital micro-ammeter

Formula (Law of Malus)

When completely plane-polarized light of intensity Imax is incident on an analyzer whose transmission axis makes an angle θ with that of the polarizer, the transmitted light intensity I is given by:

I = Imax cos²θ

Procedure

  1. Switch ON the LDR bias circuit with the light source OFF and record the initial dark current I0.
  2. Switch ON the light source and adjust the analyzer to θ = 0° so that polarizer and analyzer pass axes are parallel (maximum current).
  3. Part A: Rotate the analyzer from 0° to 90° in steps of 10° and record the micro-ammeter current I. Compute cosθ, cos²θ, and actual current (I − I0).
  4. Part B: Rotate the analyzer through a full circle from 0° to 360° in steps of 30° and record I and (I − I0).
  5. Plot (I − I0) against cos²θ (a straight line through the origin) and a polar graph of (I − I0) vs θ (two symmetric lobes).

Viva-Voce Questions & Answers (Click a question to reveal)

1. What is the difference between unpolarized light and plane-polarized light?
In unpolarized light, the electric field vector vibrates symmetrically in all directions perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation. In plane-polarized light, the electric field vibrations are confined to a single plane perpendicular to the propagation direction.
2. State the Law of Malus.
The Law of Malus states that when a beam of completely plane-polarized light is incident on an analyzer, the intensity of the emergent light varies directly as the square of the cosine of the angle between the transmission planes of the analyzer and the polarizer: I = Imax cos²θ.
3. What happens to the intensity of unpolarized light when it passes through an ideal polarizer?
When unpolarized light of intensity Iunpol passes through an ideal polarizer, the transmitted light becomes plane-polarized and its intensity is reduced to exactly half (Imax = Iunpol / 2), regardless of the polarizer's orientation.
4. At what angles between the polarizer and analyzer do maxima and minima occur over a full 360° rotation?
Maxima occur when the transmission axes are parallel at θ = 0°, 180°, and 360° (cos²θ = 1). Minima (extinction) occur when the axes are crossed at θ = 90° and 270° (cos²θ = 0).
5. Why do we measure and subtract the initial dark current (I0) in this experiment?
Even when the experimental lamp is turned off, stray ambient light and thermal leakage in the LDR circuit produce a small baseline current I0. Subtracting I0 from the observed current I isolates the true photocurrent caused solely by the transmitted polarized beam.
